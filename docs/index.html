WP A The premise

Every handoff in the close was manual, so errors were found late

Discovery with the (fictional) finance team traced every pain point to one cause: validation and matching lived in people's heads and spreadsheets instead of in the data flow. The design moves those checks to the point where data enters.

Before: where checks happened
Billing system→CSV export→Excel→Legacy ERP→Manual reconciliation→Manual journals→Reporting workbook→CFO
After: what was built
Source files→Validation→Accounting rules→AI suggestion + human approval→Posting→Reconciliation→Close Control Centre

WP D AI controls

The AI suggests the account. It does not get the last word.

An AI assistant proposes how to code each supplier invoice. A fixed policy decides whether that suggestion can go straight through or must go to a person. The policy was tested on 11 hand-built cases, including ones designed to break it.

1 of 11

Test cases approved automatically and wrong. These are 11 hand-built cases, several designed to break the policy, run against a mock model. They check that the safety policy works. They are not a real-world error rate.

A suggestion is sent to a person if any of these is true
  • Confidence below 0.95
  • Account or entity code that doesn't exist
  • Disagrees with the supplier's usual account
  • Amount of £10,000 or more (approval threshold)
  • Amount far outside the supplier's history
  • Picked by a 5% random audit sample

Thresholds are documented assumptions, not validated policy. Results use a mock model, so they test the safety policy, not a real model's accuracy. 09-ai/controls.md

WP G Limits

What this evidence does not show

Planted, not real

"Caught" means caught against a planted answer key. It says nothing about real, unplanned errors in a live ledger.

Small, readable scale

A few hundred rows over three monthly closes (June to August 2026), across three entities in GBP and USD.

Mock AI model

The real-model integration is written and tested but was never run live. The 11 AI cases measure the safety policy.

Outlier rule is too sensitive

It flags 7 of 49 supplier invoices (about 14%). The synthetic generator spreads amounts too evenly per supplier, which inflates this.
